Use Cases

How our services fit real program and operational needs.

From a grazing advisor managing dozens of properties to a weed district measuring treatment ROI — here is where Zubr fits.

Talk through your use case See our services

Defend the Core rangeland mapping

Who: Rancher or land manager participating in USDA WLFW Defend the Core programs.
What: Hyperspectral UAV survey to map intact core rangeland, identify invasive encroachment boundaries, and document vulnerability.
Outcome: Geospatial data products and assessment reports suitable for program submissions and management planning.

EQIP application and documentation support

Who: Producer or land manager applying for NRCS EQIP conservation practice funding.
What: Baseline assessment of invasive species presence and load; monitoring and evaluation reports tied to practice implementation.
Outcome: Documentation that supports the EQIP application process and demonstrates practice effectiveness over time.

Seasonal forage intelligence for a grazing advisor

Who: A grazing advisor, range consultant, or cooperative serving dozens or hundreds of landowners and allotments.
What: Forage Intelligence Reports across the client portfolio — forage quantity, quality (crude protein, fiber, silica), and grazing capacity by pasture.
Outcome: Standardized, white-label reports that translate imagery into grazing-days and AUMs for stocking and supplementation decisions.

Treatment ROI for a weed district

Who: Weed district, county noxious weed coordinator, or agency running a multi-site treatment program.
What: Before-and-after mapping across treatment units; recovered forage value, forage-days regained, and treatment-priority scoring.
Outcome: Documentation that supports treatment prioritization, budget justification, and break-even reporting.

Invasive species program management at scale

Who: Agency or district managing invasive annual grass encroachment across large landscapes.
What: Recurring UAV and satellite mapping; ventenata and cheatgrass distribution tracking across seasons and years.
Outcome: GIS datasets and reports that support treatment prioritization, budget justification, and program reporting.
