The product

ForageWatch is a workspace for measuring and managing forage across multiple pastures.

Land managers routinely combine pasture maps, animal numbers, field notes, laboratory results, and imagery by hand. ForageWatch is intended to bring those inputs together and keep the assumptions behind an estimate visible.

The public workflow is designed around satellite-supported biomass reconnaissance. A private workspace will add UAV and field data for higher-resolution forage quality and vegetation layers. The target measurements are biomass, crude protein/N, neutral detergent fiber (NDF), silica, and invasive annual grass occurrence, beginning with Ventenata dubia.

Technology and method

Models should be traceable back to field evidence.

ForageWatch’s technical approach connects three spatial scales. Each production layer will require documented training data, independent validation, and an uncertainty estimate appropriate to its intended use.

Scale 01

Field and laboratory reference

Vegetation samples and co-located observations provide the ground truth. Reference laboratory measurements for nitrogen, fiber, and silica anchor forage-quality calibration.

Scale 02

UAV hyperspectral and lidar

High-resolution imagery connects plot measurements to canopy structure and spectral response across a pasture. UAV data is intended for authenticated projects because it is property-specific.

Scale 03

Satellite context

Landsat and Sentinel observations provide repeat regional coverage for compatible targets such as biomass and vegetation condition. Satellite outputs remain subject to cloud, resolution, and calibration limits.

Delivery

Versioned decision layers

The product will record the source imagery, model version, observation date, and confidence information behind each result rather than presenting estimates as unqualified facts.

Company and research

Built in Montana around rangeland science and applied remote sensing.

Zubr Technologies

Based in Missoula, Montana, Zubr leads product development, geospatial engineering, calibration workflows, and commercialization for ForageWatch.

Research network

The current R&D plan draws on rangeland ecology, spatial analysis, optical engineering, UAV sensing, and reference laboratory chemistry through Montana research and technical collaborators.

Initial biological focus

The work began with Ventenata dubia, an invasive annual grass, and is expanding toward measurements that affect forage value and grazing decisions.

Research transparency

Partner roles, funding status, validation results, and model performance will be described publicly when they are confirmed. The website does not present proposed work as a deployed capability.
